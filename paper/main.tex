\begin{abstract}
RAPTOR builds a hierarchical retrieval tree by recursively clustering and summarizing
leaf chunks, but it inherits a naive token-based leaf chunker that ignores document
structure. Structure-aware chunking should help---but only when usable structure
\emph{exists}; on unstructured text (stories, transcripts), forcing an LLM to extract
sections is brittle. We (i) frame a controlled diagnostic of \emph{when and why}
structure-aware leaf chunking helps \emph{hierarchical} retrieval, and (ii) propose
\textbf{Adaptive Hybrid Chunking (\ahc{})}: a cheap, deterministic structure score routes
each document between structure-aware and token chunking, followed by a per-section
character-offset mapping and repair step that guarantees RAPTOR-sized leaves. We evaluate
on QASPER (papers), QuALITY (mixed), and NarrativeQA (stories), isolating the chunker as
the \emph{only} variable inside an otherwise-frozen RAPTOR pipeline, with the same
open-weight reader (\texttt{gpt-oss-120b} via OpenRouter) used for summarization and QA
across all arms. This draft reports the study design, the method, and a fully
implemented, offline-tested evaluation harness; quantitative results are pending and the
results tables are placeholders.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) quality depends on how documents are split into
retrievable units. RAPTOR~\cite{raptor} extends flat RAG by recursively clustering leaf
chunks and summarizing each cluster, producing a multi-level tree that is queried by a
\emph{collapsed-tree} search over all nodes. RAPTOR is agnostic to how leaves are formed
and uses a simple sentence/token splitter. While chunking quality is a known lever for
flat RAG, its interaction with \emph{hierarchical} retrieval is under-studied---that
interaction is our niche.

A tempting claim is ``LLM-parsed structure chunks improve RAPTOR.'' We argue this is both
incremental and, on unstructured corpora, likely invalid: an LLM asked to find sections in
a short story will hallucinate or collapse them. The real questions are \emph{when}
structure helps and whether a system can \emph{decide per document}. We make three claims,
each falsifiable:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1 (diagnostic).} Structure-aware leaf chunking improves RAPTOR on
  strongly-structured documents (papers) but does not help, or hurts, on weakly-structured
  documents (stories).
  \item \textbf{H2 (method).} \ahc{} matches or beats both pure baselines (\tokenarm{},
  \structarm{}) across document types while issuing LLM parses on substantially fewer
  documents than always-\structarm{}.
  \item \textbf{H3 (spectrum).} Within a mixed corpus (QuALITY), the structure score
  separates documents in a way that correlates with where structure chunking helps.
\end{itemize}

A confirmed-H1-but-null-H2 outcome is still an honest, publishable result and will be
reported as such.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Hierarchical retrieval.} RAPTOR~\cite{raptor} is our frozen backbone. \textbf{Chunking.}
Semantic, proposition, late, and layout-aware chunkers are an active area; we treat
structure-aware chunking as a \emph{baseline}, not a contribution, and study its
\emph{interaction} with tree retrieval. \textbf{Benchmarks.} We use QASPER~\cite{qasper}
(information-seeking QA over NLP papers), QuALITY~\cite{quality} (multiple-choice over
medium articles, with a HARD subset), and NarrativeQA~\cite{narrativeqa} (abstractive QA
over full stories). Embeddings use Sentence-BERT~\cite{sbert}.

\section{Method: Adaptive Hybrid Chunking}
\ahc{} has three deterministic stages; the only non-deterministic step is a single,
cached LLM parse used in structure mode.

\paragraph{Stage 1 --- Structure score (no LLM).}
We compute a score $s(d)\in[0,1]$ from surface signals over the non-empty lines of a
document $d$: the fraction of lines matching heading patterns (numbered \texttt{1.},
\texttt{2.1}; Markdown \texttt{\#}; short ALL-CAPS; \texttt{Chapter}/\texttt{Section}/%
\texttt{Appendix} keywords), the presence of a table-of-contents-like region, and the
regularity of inter-heading spacing. With $h$ the heading-line fraction, $t\in\{0,1\}$ the
TOC indicator, and $r\in[0,1]$ the spacing regularity,
\begin{equation}
s(d) \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\left(0.70\cdot\min\!\Big(1,\tfrac{h}{0.15}\Big) + 0.15\,t + 0.15\,r,\; 0,\; 1\right).
\end{equation}
The score and its raw feature vector are cached and logged for a feature ablation.

\paragraph{Stage 2 --- Route.}
If $s(d)\ge\tau$, attempt structure-aware chunking (one LLM parse); otherwise use token
chunking. The threshold $\tau$ is tuned on a \emph{dev} split and reported on a held-out
\emph{test} split (no test-set tuning).

\paragraph{Stage 3 --- Per-section validate and repair (structure mode).}
Parsed section titles are located in the raw text by \emph{character offset} (fixing the
page-collapse failure of the legacy chunker); unlocatable sections are marked, and any
section that is unmappable or exceeds the leaf-token budget is token-split. This makes
\ahc{} a true within-document hybrid and guarantees non-empty, RAPTOR-sized leaves.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Arms (only the chunker changes).}
\tokenarm{} (original RAPTOR splitter, primary baseline), \structarm{} (always-LLM
structure), \textbf{\ahc{}} (ours), \semarm{} (embedding-boundary chunking), and \flatarm{}
(FAISS over token chunks, no tree, to contextualize the value of the tree).

\paragraph{Frozen controls.}
All arms share identical SBERT \texttt{multi-qa-mpnet-base-cos-v1} embeddings, identical
seeded UMAP$+$GMM clustering, identical summarizer, identical reader, and identical
collapsed-tree retrieval with a 2000-token budget---RAPTOR's main setting. The
\emph{same} open-weight model, \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} served via OpenRouter, performs both
summarization and QA for every arm, so differences are attributable to the chunker alone.
All LLM calls are cached on disk (temperature $0$) for reproducibility under free-tier
nondeterminism.

\section{Threats to Validity}
Free-model nondeterminism and rate limits are mitigated by temperature $0$, on-disk
caching, and reporting variance across seeds. Small curated subsets motivate bootstrap CIs
and stratified sampling. The reader is held identical across arms to remove a QA-model
confound. Pretraining contamination (papers/stories may be in the model's training data)
is acknowledged. $\tau$ is tuned only on dev and reported, not cherry-picked. Claims are
scoped to RAPTOR-style hierarchical pipelines.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented the design and a working, reproducible harness for a controlled study of
leaf chunking in hierarchical RAG, and \ahc{}, a deterministic per-document router with
per-section repair. The contribution is a characterization of \emph{when} structure helps
plus an adaptive method that aims to match or beat both pure baselines at lower parse cost.
Empirical validation is the immediate next step.
